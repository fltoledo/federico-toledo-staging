The format

A workshop among peers

Every edition follows a format inspired by LAWST (Los Altos Workshop on Software Testing), the same one the WOPR (Workshop on Performance and Reliability) has used since 2003. It has been refined over more than twenty years so that a group of practitioners can truly learn from each other’s experiences.

The essentials

  • Small group: up to 20 participants. We tried 25 and it was harder to keep the conversations in order.
  • Experience reports: to take part you propose a first-hand experience related to the theme of the edition.
  • Short presentations, long discussions: each report takes at most 30 minutes; questions have no time limit. That is why nobody knows in advance who will get to present.
  • Full commitment: three days, 9 am to 6 pm, in person in the city of the edition.
  • Free: the workshop has no cost. It does not cover travel or lodging, but coffee, drinks and snacks are provided during breaks.
  • Diversity: when selecting, we look for the best proposals and also for the most diverse group possible.

A space of trust

We aim for an environment of trust, respect and safety. What is shared is meant to enrich the group and the community, but we are very careful with confidential information: at the start of the workshop we make an explicit agreement about what can leave the room. Occasionally we take a photo to share the experience on social media or in an article.

K-Card facilitation

Facilitation has two roles. The Content Owner decides which report is presented at each moment and gives each person advance notice. The facilitator keeps the conversation in order using K-Cards: colored cards that participants raise to ask for the floor.

  • Green · Propose a topic

    Raise it to open a new topic or a question different from the one being discussed. Very useful at the end of a presentation to spark new discussions.

  • Yellow · Add to the topic

    Raise it to add a comment or question on the current topic. If your point drifts too far from it, use a green card instead. Don’t worry: every topic gets its turn.

  • Red · Speak now

    Raise it when you need to speak right away: the current conversation stops. Use it with care; if it is abused, the facilitator may take it away.

The dynamic is explained at the workshop, but it helps to arrive with an idea of how it works. Leandro Melendez (Señor Performo) explains it in a video of less than 4 minutes (in Spanish), based on WOPR 2022 in Uruguay.

To see it in action, watch this explanation and simulation of an experience report on the Codigio channel (in Spanish), with Antonio Jiménez, who has taken part in several workshops, and Jenifer Sánchez, co-organizer of the 2024 edition in Colombia.

Typical agenda

This is a reference and is adjusted for each edition.

Day 0 · 7 pm

Dinner at a local restaurant. Most people arrive the day before, and it is a good way to start getting to know each other.

Days 1, 2 and 3

TimeActivity
8:30Breakfast and networking
9:00Start. Day 1: welcome, participation agreement and introduction to the theme. Days 2 and 3: check-in.
10:30Coffee break
12:30Lunch
15:30Coffee break in the room
17:30Check-out and close of the day

At the start of each day and after lunch we run short energizers (games, speed dating) to keep the energy up and get to know each other better. On day 3, after lunch, the active report is wrapped up and those who did not get to present give a kind of elevator pitch. We close with a round of thanks and a toast.
